# ADR-007: Use an Application-Level Append-Only Audit Log

**Status:** Accepted
**Superseded in part:** ADR-011 narrows this log to successful transport publications only (publisher and publication time), with 30-day retention. Edit, announcement, and login/logout records are not logged.
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Project:** University Transport Information Portal

## Related drivers

- **ASR-06 — Audit history and recovery:** Edits and publications must be retained in an auditable history for 30 days with actor attribution. Recovery is limited to previously published data, not drafts.
- **ASR-03 — Draft/publish separation:** Draft and published versions must remain distinct, and public users must never see unpublished drafts.

## Context

The portal must maintain a reliable history of changes to transport data and announcements. Each edit and publication must be recorded with actor attribution and retained for 30 days.

The system must also support recovery, but recovery is restricted to previously published data. Drafts must never become recovery targets.

The architecture must therefore provide an auditable record of changes while supporting recovery without introducing unnecessary event-processing complexity.

## Options considered

| Option | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated append-only audit table written by the application | Provides an audit history and can be written transactionally alongside edits and publications | Requires application-level audit-writing logic |
| Full event sourcing | Provides a comprehensive event-based history | More powerful and complex than this domain requires |

## Decision

Adopt an **application-level, append-only audit log**, written transactionally alongside each edit and publication.

- The Audit module owns the append-only edit/publish history.
- Each audit entry must include actor attribution.
- Audit history must be retained for 30 days.
- Audit entries must be written transactionally alongside the corresponding edit or publication.
- Recovery must point a published pointer back to a previously retained published version.
- Recovery must never target a draft.

## Rationale

Full event sourcing could support the audit requirements, but it introduces more complexity than this domain requires.

An application-level append-only audit log provides the required history while allowing audit entries to be written transactionally alongside edits and publications.

Using a published pointer to recover a previously retained published version preserves the draft/publish separation and prevents drafts from becoming recovery targets.

This approach provides the required traceability and recovery behavior without adopting a full event-sourced architecture.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Edits and publications have an auditable history.
- Audit entries include actor attribution.
- Transactional writing keeps audit records alongside the corresponding changes.
- The 30-day retention requirement is supported.
- Recovery can target previously published versions without restoring drafts.

### Negative
- Application operations must include audit-writing logic.
- Audit writes must be coordinated transactionally with edits and publications.
- Recovery requires correct handling of published-version pointers.

### Risks
- If an edit or publication is committed without its corresponding audit entry, the audit history becomes incomplete.
- Incorrect recovery logic could point to a draft rather than a previously published version.
- Incorrect retention handling could cause audit history to fall outside the required 30-day window.

### Revisit when
- The application-level audit log cannot reliably meet audit or recovery requirements.
- A reviewed, ASR-based justification supports adopting a different audit architecture.

## Verification

- Verify that every edit and publication creates an audit entry.
- Verify that audit entries are written transactionally alongside the corresponding operation.
- Verify that each audit entry records actor attribution.
- Verify that audit history is retained for 30 days.
- Test retrieval of audit history for an entity over a specified date range.
- Test recovery to a previously retained published version.
- Verify that recovery cannot target drafts.
- Test audit recovery correctness as part of recovery testing.
- Confirm that the implementation does not introduce full event sourcing without an ASR-based justification.
