# ADR-006: Implement Offline Caching Through a PWA Service Worker

**Status:** Accepted
**Current profile:** ADR-011 selects Workbox, limits periodic synchronization to when the app is open, and excludes active urgent banners from offline cache.
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Project:** University Transport Information Portal

## Related drivers

- **ASR-04 — Offline availability and client state handling:** The client must distinguish stale, offline, and service/data outage states, retain local cached data for 24 hours, and perform background synchronization approximately every minute.
- **ASR-08 — Platform compatibility:** The client must work on mobile, laptop, and desktop, with mobile prioritized.

## Context

Students and the public must be able to access transport information even when their device temporarily loses connectivity.

The client must distinguish three different conditions:

- **Stale:** Previously cached information is available but may no longer be current.
- **Offline:** The device has no network connectivity.
- **Service/data outage:** The device may have connectivity, but the service or requested data is unavailable.

The architecture requires 24-hour local cache retention and approximately one-minute background synchronization. It must also support mobile, laptop, and desktop devices.

The decision is how to provide local persistence and synchronization without introducing unnecessary client-side complexity.

## Options considered

| Option | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| No client-side cache | Simpler client implementation | Fails the offline requirement outright |
| PWA Service Worker | Supports local persistence and synchronization; matches the tri-state requirement directly | Requires client-side cache and synchronization logic |

## Decision

Adopt a **PWA with a Service Worker and IndexedDB/Cache Storage** for local persistence.

- Store transport-data payloads locally.
- Store synchronization timestamps locally.
- Retain cached data for 24 hours.
- Implement a synchronization mechanism that runs approximately every minute.
- Distinguish stale, offline, and service/data outage states using client-side logic.
- Keep this logic entirely on the client and use locally stored timestamps to determine the relevant state.

## Rationale

Not providing a client-side cache would fail the offline requirement outright.

A Service Worker approach directly supports the required client-side persistence and tri-state behavior. IndexedDB/Cache Storage provides the selected storage approach for transport-data payloads and synchronization timestamps.

Keeping state-handling logic on the client allows the application to distinguish stale, offline, and outage states using locally stored information rather than requiring server round-trips for this logic.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Previously retrieved transport information remains available locally.
- The client can support the required 24-hour cache retention.
- Local timestamps support stale-state determination.
- The Service Worker approach supports the required synchronization mechanism.
- The approach supports the portal's mobile-prioritized PWA architecture.

### Negative
- The client must manage local persistence and synchronization.
- The client must correctly distinguish three different operating states.
- Cached information may be outdated, so the interface must communicate its state accurately.

### Risks
- Incorrect timestamp handling could cause the client to misrepresent stale information.
- Synchronization failures could leave cached transport information outdated.
- Incorrect state handling could confuse an offline device with a service/data outage.

### Revisit when
- The selected client-side approach cannot reliably meet the 24-hour retention, approximately one-minute synchronization, or tri-state requirements.
- A reviewed, ASR-based justification supports changing the client caching approach.

## Verification

- Verify that the PWA uses a Service Worker for the selected offline approach.
- Verify that transport-data payloads and synchronization timestamps are stored using IndexedDB/Cache Storage.
- Test that cached data is retained for 24 hours.
- Test synchronization at approximately one-minute intervals.
- Test that the client distinguishes stale, offline, and service/data outage states correctly.
- Verify that state-handling logic uses locally stored timestamps and does not depend on server round-trips.
- Test offline, stale, and outage behavior across mobile, laptop, and desktop devices.
- Confirm that the client does not expose unpublished draft data through local caching.
